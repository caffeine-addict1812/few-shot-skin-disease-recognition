Recognising skin diseases it has never seen, from five photos.

Most skin diseases are rare, with too few photos to train a classifier. We trained a network to compare photos instead. Shown one to five examples of a disease it never trained on, it labels new photos by which example group they sit closest to.

The problem

Rare diseases have too few photos to learn from

SD-198 holds 6,584 clinical photos of 198 skin diseases, taken with ordinary cameras: varied light, framing and skin tones. Many diseases are rare. 70 of them have only 10 to 19 photos.

A standard classifier needs hundreds of photos per disease and only knows the diseases it was trained on. So we make the test honest: every disease with fewer than 20 photos is held out completely. The model trains on 101 diseases, picks its checkpoint on 27 others, and is tested on the 70 it has never seen.

Each test is an episode: pick 5 never-seen diseases, show the model 1 or 5 example photos of each, then ask it to label new photos of those diseases.

Method

Learn to compare, not to memorise

Prototypical network (Snell et al., 2017). A ResNet-18 turns every photo into 512 numbers. For each disease in an episode, the average of its example photos is its prototype. A new photo gets the label of the nearest prototype by squared distance. There is no classifier layer, so new diseases need no retraining.

Episodic training. Training repeats that exact test on the 101 training diseases: thousands of small 5-disease quizzes, each pulling photos of the same disease together and pushing different diseases apart.

Recipe: ResNet-18 with ImageNet weights, 4,000 episodes (5 diseases, 5 examples, 15 queries each; 4 episodes per step), AdamW with backbone learning rate 1e-05, cosine schedule, bf16, random crops, flips, rotation and colour jitter. The checkpoint with the best accuracy on 600 fixed validation episodes is kept.

Baseline: the same network and prototype rule with no fine-tuning (plain ImageNet features), scored on the identical test episodes. Fine-tuning only counts if it beats this.

What went wrong first

The planned schedule memorised the training diseases

The original plan, 20,000 episodes at learning rate 0.0001, overfit. On unseen validation diseases it peaked at 78.2% and then fell to 73.8%, while accuracy on the training diseases climbed to 99.4%. A five-setting sweep, judged on validation diseases only, led to the final recipe: 4,000 episodes at learning rate 1e-05. The test diseases were not looked at until the end.

Results

85.9% on diseases it has never seen

The plain ImageNet network is already a strong start, so the honest measure is how many of its mistakes the trained model removes: 28% of them with five photos per disease, 22% with one. On the same 600 test episodes it beats the ImageNet network in 449 and loses 129 (one photo each).

How robust is it

The result holds when we change things

Same run, another GPU≤ 0.18 pp

Retraining the seed-42 run on a second machine lands within 0.18 pp on every test setting. That is the noise floor for comparing runs.

Other training diseases≤ 0.3 pp

Two more train/validation partitions of the 128 common diseases, 3 seeds each: 5-way accuracy moves by at most 0.3 pp (86.0% mean for 5 photos each).

A bigger backbone+0.1 to +1.3 pp

ResNet-50 (2x the parameters) helps only a little: the difference is clear of noise in 5-way 1-shot and 2-way 1-shot only.

Did we check it

Every number was re-derived independently

  • Numbers reproduce. Recomputed through a separate code path on CPU: every result matches within 0.08 pp.
  • Not tuned to 600 lucky episodes. On 5,000 new test episodes, accuracy stays within 1.1 pp.
  • The metric is sound. Shuffling the answer labels drops accuracy to chance (20% and 50%).
  • No test peeking. Each checkpoint was chosen on validation diseases only; training logs hold no test records.
  • No watermark shortcut. Masking the grey source strip at the bottom of the photos changes accuracy by at most 0.9 pp.
  • Fine-tuning really helps. The gain over ImageNet features is clear of noise in all four settings (paired bootstrap over episodes).
  • Same-patient photos. SD-198 holds re-shoots of the same patient. Removing them costs about 1.2 pp at 1 photo each (at most 7.2 pp); the gain over ImageNet features is unchanged.

Limits and next steps

What this does not show

This is a class project, not a clinical tool. The test diseases have only 10 to 19 photos, so each episode uses 5 query photos per disease, and confidence intervals are about ±1 pp. Plain ImageNet features are already strong (80.2%), which limits how much fine-tuning can add.

The backbone and method are deliberately simple, so the numbers are not comparable with published SD-198 results that use larger models.

Next: dermatology-specific or self-supervised pretraining, which may matter more than a bigger network (ResNet-50 added at most 1.3 pp); cross-dataset tests on dermoscopy (Derm7pt); a patient-aware test sampler.
